## 2. Verdict

**H06: REJECTED** on all four criteria. In-sample expectancy is **−2.93 pips with a 95% CI of
[−4.27, −1.54]** — significantly *negative*, i.e. this rule does not merely fail to find an edge,
it reliably donates the spread. 117 of 222 in-sample exits were stop-outs.

## 3. The pattern across all six hypotheses

| experiment | mechanism | direction | result | significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXP-0001 | D1 z-score fade | reversion | +68 pips (n=14) | inconclusive, inside random band |
| EXP-0002 | same, 26y × 5 pairs | reversion | +5.81 pips (n=94) | indistinguishable from random (mean +2.79) |
| EXP-0003 | H1 fade, intraday | reversion | IS −1.66 (n=114) | CI spans 0, control flips sign |
| EXP-0004 | H1 breakout | momentum | **IS −2.93, CI [−4.27, −1.54]** (n=222) | significantly negative |

**Both directions lose after costs.** Fading loses ~1.7 pips/trade, chasing loses ~2.9 pips/trade,
against a round-trip cost of 1.3 pips. That is the signature of a market where, at this scale,
cost plus adverse selection absorbs the available movement — not of a rule that needs one more tweak.

## 4. What the numbers imply about feasibility

- Median H1 ATR ≈ 5.9 pips (EURGBP). A 1.3-pip round trip is **22% of one bar's range**.
- To be profitable you need to capture movement systematically larger than the range you pay for.
  Across four structurally different rules, none did.
- The cost model is still an **assumption** (no measured H1 spread). If the real spread is 2.0 pips
  the picture only gets worse; it would have to be ~0.3 pips to change the conclusion.
