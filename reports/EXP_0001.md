## 2. Random-entry benchmark (2,000 simulations, same count/hold/cost)

| statistic | value |

| mean net expectancy of random shorts | **+2.02 pips** |
| 5th percentile | −111.3 pips |

The observed +68.33 pips sits **inside** the random band. In plain words: we cannot show
this is better than shorting at random dates with the same holding period.

## 3. Why the test failed — diagnostics

1. **The sample requirement was not met.** Pre-registered minimum: ≥30 in-sample entries.
   Reality: **25 signal episodes in 10 years, 14 traded** (11 skipped because a position was
   already open). ~1.4 entries/year. With 14 trades, no CI can exclude zero.
2. **The validation block cannot be tested** (n=3 vs required ≥10). Any conclusion from it,
   positive or negative, is noise.
3. **Concentration.** Episodes: 2022 → 10, 2025 → 7, 2018 → 3, 2019 → 2, 2020 → 1, 2024 → 2.
   The rule is essentially a bet on crisis-years happening.
4. **Cost is not the problem.** Cost = 1.3 pips vs gross expectancy 62.9 pips (2%). The edge,
   such as it is, is not eaten by spread.
5. **The two stop-losses dominate the drawdown.** 2020-03-16 (−304 pips, COVID) and
   2022-09-01 (−185 pips, gilt/LDI crisis) = the 3×ATR stop being hit in vol spikes.
   Regime risk, not execution cost, is the binding constraint.

## 4. Verdict

**H01: NOT SUPPORTED.** Not "falsified" — *unfalsifiable with this data*, because 14 trades
cannot separate skill from luck. Per the pre-registered criteria, the test fails on
"(b) bootstrap CI includes 0" and on the sample requirement.

**H01 will not be re-tuned.** Lowering z to 1.5, or shortening the hold, or widening the stop
would be data mining on the same 2,592 bars and would destroy the one honest test we have left.

## 5. What this experiment did establish (positive carry-forward)

- The engine is trustworthy: next-bar fills, no look-ahead, costs applied, 13 unit tests.
- The cost model is measured, not guessed: 1.3 pips per round trip is 2% of the gross move.
- The binding constraint is now **statistical power**, not data cleaning, not cost, not code.

## 6. Options to raise power (owner decision required)

| option | how it raises n | cost | risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Longer history** — export EURGBP D1 back to ~2003 from MT5 | ×2.5 trades | free, owner does the export | older regimes may not apply |
| **B. Cross-sectional basket** — same frozen rule on EURGBP + EURCHF + AUDNZD + CADCHF + EURUSD, equal risk per instrument | ×4–5 trades | 1 session | pairs are correlated ⇒ less independent than it looks |
| **C. Higher frequency** — run the same logic on H1 with a proportionally scaled threshold | ×3–6 trades | needs clean H1 + spread model | costs per trade rise 5–10× |
| D. Lower the threshold as a NEW hypothesis ID (e.g. H05, z ≥ 1.5) with the locked OOS as the only honest test | ×2 | 1 session | highest false-discovery risk; OOS gets consumed |

**My recommendation: A + B together, and leave D alone.** More history and a diversified basket
raise n without touching the threshold, which keeps the OOS block unused and honest.
